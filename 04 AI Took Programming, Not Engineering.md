# AI Took Programming, Not Engineering. Will It Do the Same to Other Roles?

*Originally published on LinkedIn: <https://lnkd.in/p/gz73NR4J>*

The first three posts used a code-versus-agent design lens: who calls whom, who owns the control loop, who waits, who consumes semantic judgment, and what limits a wrong decision.

That lens helps rule arrangements in or out. But it also exposes the other side: some are already doing real work.

Software development is the clearest case.

AI moved from completing lines to generating code, then to agents that inspect repositories, call tools, run tests, and revise their work. Evidence is mixed: a field study of 4,867 developers found 26% more completed tasks; a smaller METR study—16 experienced open-source developers working 246 issues—found they took 19% longer while believing AI had sped them up. Opposite results, same fact: software-development duties are moving.

Anthropic's December 2024 engineering guide marked the shift publicly. It described agents as emerging in production and coding as promising because code can be executed, tested, and measured—while human review remained crucial for broader system requirements.

Now take that trajectory to its loudest conclusion: can AI replace everyone?

Tycoon calls itself an operating system for one-person companies. Its founder says she replaced herself with an AI CEO, Astra, with AI employees across coding, marketing, and operations. She made the bet with her own company.

But I do not think whole-role substitution is the fundamental enterprise pattern.

A role is a bundle of duties. The split is between duties AI can perform—those with an expressible target and checkable output—and the judgment and organizational accountability that remain human.

A role is therefore the unit to decompose, not the unit to substitute. Duties are the unit to allocate between people and AI.

Across enterprise information systems: business analyst. Solution architect. Tester. Data steward. Operations. Change manager. Project manager.

I am not claiming that this split is already established across those roles. Agentic AI is young, and enterprises are still experimenting. Software development has simply made the possibility visible first.

Is it a technically favorable exception—or the first glimpse of what will happen to every other role in enterprise information systems?
