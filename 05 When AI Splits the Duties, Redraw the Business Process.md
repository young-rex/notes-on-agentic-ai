# When AI Splits the Duties, Redraw the Business Process

*Originally published on LinkedIn: <https://lnkd.in/p/g9UUxGaM>*

In the previous post, I argued that a role is a bundle of duties. The role is the unit to decompose; duties are the unit to allocate between people and AI.

But transformation cannot stop at the boundary of an individual role. Roles do not operate alone.

A business process connects them. In Business Process Model and Notation (BPMN), participants appear as pools, roles commonly appear as lanes, activities sit inside them, and flows connect the work. Together, the model shows handoffs, reviews, decisions, and controls.

If AI materially changes what happens inside several lanes, why assume the flows stay where they are?

An agent may:

- remove a handoff,
- create a verification step, or
- accelerate upstream production until downstream judgment becomes the bottleneck.

One role may start receiving work that is faster, more variable, or already partly decided. Even a role that never directly uses AI may change because the work reaching it has changed.

That was my instinct. I went looking for what AI research says about effects beyond individual tasks.

The article “Generative AI and the Social Fabric of Organizations” argues that a task-by-task view misses the longer-term effect: GenAI can reroute how people cross paths and exchange expertise, trust, and support.

A systematic review of 48 studies identified recurring structural themes—organizational redesign, human-AI collaboration, and team restructuring—and procedural themes including data-driven decision-making, operational efficiency, and automation.

Neither study gives us a future process map. But both support the same direction: the effect does not remain contained inside the task where AI was introduced.

The organizational mechanism is not new: when the division of work changes, processes must be redesigned. AI is the new trigger.

So here is my prediction for enterprise AI transformation:

**Role decomposition → Human/AI duty allocation → Process recomposition**

A duty successfully moved to AI is not yet a successful transformation. After changing the duties inside the lanes, we need to review every flow around them: handoffs, decisions, and controls.

If the process was designed for the old division of duties, why assume it remains right for the new one?
